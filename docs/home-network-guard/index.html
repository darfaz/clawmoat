The WSJ pattern

Cheap connected devices got trusted network access. Then they quietly worked for someone else.

The lesson is bigger than IoT. Homes and laptops are filling with autonomous systems: Android boxes, cameras, routers, cron jobs, AI agents, MCP servers, and browser automation. ClawMoat watches the systems that can act without you.

Find every device

Inventory local devices from neighbor/ARP tables, then classify known, unknown, and suspicious devices.

Flag risky exposure

Detect exposed Telnet, Android Debug Bridge, UPnP, router-management ports, and other botnet-friendly surfaces.

Spot proxy indicators

When DNS or router logs are provided, flag proxy-like domains, datacenter-heavy egress, tunnels, and residential-proxy patterns.

Explain the fix

Plain English remediation: isolate, move to guest/IoT network, disable ADB/Telnet, block outbound traffic, or remove the device.

CLI MVP

Detection first. Persistent watch now. Blocking comes through router and DNS integrations.

ClawMoat can scan once or save a baseline and alert when a new or riskier device appears. Actual quarantine still requires Pi-hole, AdGuard Home, OpenWRT, UniFi, pfSense, OPNsense, or router/firewall APIs.

ClawMoat does for AI agents and home devices what home networks never had for cheap IoT: runtime supervision before trusted systems become someone else’s infrastructure.
